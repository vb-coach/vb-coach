vb-coach

Physics models that turn what volleyball coaches say about a ball — a set that “stands up” (立得住), a ball with a “head” (球头), a serve that “floats” (飘) — into quantities you can compute, plot and test.

Wiki GitHub Quick start

Questions

  1. How do a setter's release position, speed, height and spin decide how well the attacker can hit?
  2. Attackers differ in height, reach and contact point. Which set suits each of them?
  3. How much does a float serve actually move, can it be predicted, and when does it move most?
  4. For the same release, which of three attacks — high ball, half-high, or shoot — fits this attacker best?

What is built

Trajectory engine
Gravity, drag and Magnus force integrated with RK45. Drag covers both the subcritical regime and the drag crisis; a stochastic model adds the lateral forces behind float serves.
Attack scoring
Attackers modeled from height, standing and jump reach, and level, with seven presets from amateur to elite. Each set gets a score in [0, 1] for high, half-high and shoot attacks from positions 4 and 2, with dwell time, vertical speed at contact and lateral drift as diagnostics.
Visualization
Side views and 3D plots, Plotly animations with adjustable playback, a metric Three.js court seen from a player's eye height, and a Streamlit app for attacks and float serves.
Float-serve study
Float serves sit between 13 and 20 m/s, move most near a critical Reynolds number of about 1.5–2.5 × 105, and land with about 30 cm of sideline scatter.

Quick start

uv sync
uv run streamlit run app.py
# then open http://localhost:8501

The app has an attack scene and a float-serve scene. Scripts under examples/ produce the static figures and animated HTML views.

Limits

  • Scores are priors from coaching intuition and physics, not calibrated against match data. A 200-rally video annotation study is planned to fill that gap.
  • Defaults describe an amateur male attacker (178 cm tall, 295 cm jump reach, 280 cm contact point).
